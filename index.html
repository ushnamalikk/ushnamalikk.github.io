About

I study how AI systems shape what people are able to know. My work audits AI intermediaries in information seeking, measures what they remove or alter before a user sees a result, and examines how people calibrate trust in them.

My interests span human–AI interaction, AI-mediated search, algorithmic auditing, and computational social science. Before Iowa I completed my BSc at LUMS, working with Ihsan Ayyub Qazi, Ayesha Ali, and Zafar Ayyub Qazi.

Prompting, Oversight, and Adoption: Physicians' Use of Large Language Models for Diagnostic Reasoning in an LMIC
U. Malik*, L. I. Ahmad*, et al. · CHI 2026
Mixed-methods study of vignette interaction logs and 12 physician interviews, examining prompting strategies, verification behaviour, and calibrated trust in a resource-constrained clinical setting.

The Assistant Erased You: Measuring Loss of Authorship Signals in AI-Mediated Communication
U. Malik, M. S. Awan · CSCW 2026 Workshop on the Broader Impacts of GenAI in Communication
Introduces the Idiolect Erasure Rate, a measure of how much authorship signal survives an AI writing assistant. Attribution accuracy drops by up to 66.5 points while most rewrites still evade AI-text detectors.
